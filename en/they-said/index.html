They said.

The 17 July statement gives the floor to the three signatories of the decision. It is, to our knowledge, the most unintentionally funny document of the sailing year. Let us read it together — exact quotes, full attribution, and the original linked so every word can be checked.

Statement no. 1

The owner

Marc Feuillée, managing director of Groupe Figaro, opens proceedings: La Solitaire is a "sporting heritage", and his responsibility is to "preserve what makes it singular". Excellent news — that is precisely the thesis of our tribune. We find ourselves in full agreement with Le Figaro.

One small question, then: what makes La Solitaire singular? Strictly identical boats, and a door open to all. Is that all? That is all. And what does the decision abolish? The identical boats and the open door. The managing director has thus defined his mission in his first sentence and signed its opposite in his last — within the same paragraph, which is admirably time-efficient.

Note also the "boldness" the newspaper claims for itself in embracing the great changes of the age. Le Figaro leading the revolution: a genuine first in two centuries of French press history. The founders would be so proud.

Statement no. 2

The organiser

Hervé Favre, president of OC Sport Pen Duick, promises an event "faithful to the sporting principles" that built its reputation over more than fifty years. Let us take inventory of this faithfulness. Strict equality of equipment — abolished. Accessibility to every budget — abolished. Openness to rookies — abolished. Not one founding principle of 1970 or 1991 survives the decision.

This, then, is faithfulness of a very high order: it survives the disappearance of everything it is faithful to. We knew the Ship of Theseus; here is the Race of Theseus — the fleet, the format, the budget and the competitors have all been replaced, but rest assured, the principles are intact. They must be stored somewhere at head office, in Lorient.

Statement no. 3

The beneficiary

Sébastien Rogues, co-president of the Ocean Fifty class, deserves our gratitude: he is the only one of the three telling the truth, and he tells all of it. The proposal came from OC Sport? He confirms it. It is a "revolution"? He confirms that too — while the floor above swears by continuity, though apparently no one proofread the whole document. An "opportunity" to consolidate his class's identity? Consolidating one class's identity with the name of another class's race: it took some nerve to phrase it. He did.

And he saves the best for last: La Solitaire will be one component of the Ocean Fifty championship, allowing skippers to "prepare for the Route du Rhum, the pinnacle" of their campaigns. Translation: the queen race becomes the training race — and the pinnacle in question is organised by OC Sport itself. The contractor has turned its client's crown jewel into a warm-up for its own event, and the client signed underneath. In the trade, this is called a joint statement. In every other trade, it is called something else.

Summary

Three signatures, three versions

Let us sum up this remarkable document: the owner preserves a singularity he abolishes, the organiser stays faithful to principles he repeals, and the beneficiary — the only relaxed man in the story — calmly describes the revolution, the appropriation and the demotion. Three signatures, three versions: two incompatible with the facts, and the third incompatible with the other two.

When you want to know what a decision really is, you do not listen to those dressing it up. You listen to the one unwrapping it.

They wrote "heritage", "faithfulness", "legacy". Then they signed. It is the only point on which all three agree.
